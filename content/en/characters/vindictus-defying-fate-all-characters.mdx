## How Character Swapping, Followers, and Campfires Work

One of the smartest design choices in *Vindictus: Defying Fate* is that you are never locked into a hero. Whenever you rest at a campfire, you can change which character you're controlling, and you can also change which character your follower is using. That single feature turns the roster into a toolbox instead of a commitment.

| System | How It Works | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Character swap | Change your controlled hero at any campfire | Test playstyles without restarting your run |
| Follower swap | Change your companion's hero at any campfire | Reshape team dynamics on the fly |
| Per-character skill tree | Ability Points spent on one hero only | Rewards specialization |
| Shared general skill tree | Ability Points benefit all heroes and followers | Progress is never wasted |
| Ability Point sources | Found in chests alongside currency and materials | Exploration directly powers you up |

That shared general skill tree is the detail most new players miss. Because those nodes apply to every character — including whichever hero your follower is playing — you can invest there with zero fear of regret. Character-specific nodes are where you commit.

## Every Playable Character in Detail

### Lann — Twin Swords

Lann is the poster hero for speed. He dual-wields twin swords and leans on fast, flashy techniques that chain hits together in long strings. His core kit — **Cross Assault**, **Windmill**, **Raging Fever**, and **Fatal Fury** — points toward a hero who wants to stay glued to a target and keep the combo counter climbing.

If you enjoy games where the fun comes from execution and momentum, Lann is your entry point. The trade-off, based on player experience with the alpha, is forgiveness: fast heroes usually punish sloppy positioning harder than tanky ones. Expect to learn boss patterns the honest way.

### Fiona — Longsword and Shield

Fiona is the roster's anchor. She fights with a longsword and shield and is built to be strong on both sides of the exchange, using **Shield Bash**, **Focal Point**, **Bird of Prey**, and **Shield Charge**. Where Lann wants to never stop moving, Fiona wants to choose the right moment.

For players coming from Soulslike or action-RPG backgrounds, Fiona is usually the smoothest first main. Blocking and countering give you a way to slow a fight down, observe a boss, and then punish the opening you created. Community reports consistently place her among the most forgiving heroes for a first playthrough.

### Karok — Massive Pillar

Karok is a half-giant warrior who dominates the battlefield with a massive pillar. His core skills — **Shoulder Check**, **Whirlwind**, **Kodiak Rush**, and **Earthquake** — read like a list of ways to rearrange a crowd. He trades speed for reach, stagger, and raw area pressure.

Karok is the pick for anyone who finds small, precise weapons stressful. His attacks commit hard, but they also hit wide, which makes clearing groups and breaking enemy poise far more forgiving than his size suggests. Just budget your dodge timing: heavy heroes in this franchise tend to have longer recovery windows.

### Delia — Bastard Sword

Delia wields a **bastard sword**, not twin blades, and her kit is built around deliberate, elegant combo strings. Her core skills — **Fearless Slash**, **Bloom Waltz**, **White Rose**, and **Blue Rose Crush** — suggest a hero who rewards players for learning sequences rather than mashing.

If a description doesn't match the listed weapon type, trust the weapon type. Delia's identity is the two-handed bastard sword and the flowing, dance-like strings that come with it.

## Ability Points, Skill Trees, and Weapon Upgrades

Progression in *Vindictus: Defying Fate* runs on two tracks: leveling and Ability Points. Characters gain stats naturally as they level, while Ability Points come primarily from chests you find in the world. Opening a chest typically yields an Ability Point, some currency, and occasionally upgrade materials such as Charcoal.

| Step | Where | What You Need | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Collect Ability Points | Field chests | Exploration | Usually 1 AP per chest plus currency |
| 2. Spend AP on skill trees | Character menu | Ability Points | Shared tree first is the safe play |
| 3. Craft or upgrade weapons | Village blacksmith | Currency + materials (e.g., Charcoal) | No need to equip the weapon |
| 4. Change your look | Outfits area | Unlocked visuals | Stats are already applied |

The weapon system deserves a highlight. When you craft or upgrade a weapon at the blacksmith, its stats are added to your character automatically — you do not have to equip it. Visuals are handled separately in the Outfits area, which means you can chase the best stats without being forced into an ugly weapon. That's a genuinely player-friendly loop, and it means a trip to the village blacksmith is never wasted time.

Do note that finalized starting attributes (health, attack power, speed, defense, critical rate) were still placeholders in the alpha documentation. Don't build a plan around early stat tables — they will move.

## Which Character Should You Play?

Rather than asking which hero is strongest, ask which one matches how you already like to play. The roster is small enough that you can realistically test all four in a single session thanks to campfire swapping.

| If You Enjoy... | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fast combos and constant pressure | Lann | Twin swords reward aggression and chaining |
| Blocking, timing, and counterattacks | Fiona | Shield tools let you control the pace |
| Big hits and crowd clearing | Karok | Pillar sweeps and staggers cover wide areas |
| Learning elegant, technical sequences | Delia | Bastard sword strings reward practice |

A few practical tips from player experience:

- **Swap at every campfire.** There's no cost, and ten minutes with a hero tells you more than any tier list.
- **Invest in the shared tree early.** It benefits every hero and follower, so it's the lowest-risk AP spend.
- **Open every chest you pass.** Ability Points are the real currency of character growth, not gold.
- **Match your follower to your weakness.** If you're playing a slow hero, a fast follower balances the team.
- **Revisit your main after upgrading weapons.** Stats apply automatically, which can meaningfully change how a hero feels.
