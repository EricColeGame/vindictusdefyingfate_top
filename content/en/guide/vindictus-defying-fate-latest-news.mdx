### Why the FGT Matters More Than a Trailer

A Focus Group Test is not a demo. It is a controlled environment where developers watch how real players handle difficulty curves, input timing, and character variety. For a combat-driven game, that feedback loop directly shapes boss design and animation timing — the two things that make or break a dodge-and-counter system.

## Combat Guide: Every Hit Counts, Every Action Matters

The store page is blunt about the design philosophy: read your enemy's attacks and strike back for maximum impact. If you plan to play at launch, you can start building the right habits now.

| Mechanic | Why It Matters | How to Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perfect dodge | Avoids damage and opens a punish window | Learn one enemy pattern at a time instead of mashing roll |
| Counters | Convert defense into burst damage | Watch for the tell before the swing, not the swing itself |
| Heavy strikes | Deliver maximum impact on staggered foes | Save them for confirmed openings, not guesses |
| Character command inputs | Unique skills and combo routes per hero | Pick one character and master their full input list |
| Third-person positioning | Camera and spacing decide fights | Fight at mid-range until you know a boss's reach |

A few habits separate players who survive from players who restart:

- **Treat the first attempt as reconnaissance.** Do not chase damage; learn the attack rhythm.
- **Practice dodge timing without attacking.** Ten minutes of pure evasion teaches more than an hour of button mashing.
- **Commit to one character early.** Diverse command inputs mean each hero has a genuinely different learning curve.
- **Use a controller if you can.** The developers explicitly recommend Xbox controllers, and action games with precise counter windows tend to feel better on a gamepad.

Community reports from similar action RPGs consistently show that players who practice defensive timing first progress faster than those who chase damage numbers. That pattern is likely to hold here.
